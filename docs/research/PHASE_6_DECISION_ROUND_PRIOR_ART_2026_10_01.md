## Remaining question and required comparison

The narrow candidate question is whether allocating a finite resident program store jointly to ordinary job code, allowed interruption sites, checkpoint metadata, and executable save/restore sequences changes the achievable blocking/overhead frontier on a small shared scratchpad accelerator. The experiment uses a 32 KiB program capacity and a 32 KiB data scratchpad with one shared 64-bit port.

This constraint does not by itself establish novelty. DERCA already considers platform constraints and state movement. Its scalability discussion explicitly charges 24 bytes of on-chip metadata per non-preemptive region. A defensible contribution would require demonstrating an additional mechanism or result beyond a straightforward resource-budgeted application of its ideas, and checking the full algorithm/artifact before claiming the finite-program formulation is absent. The present bounded policy comparison uses ordinary boundaries and uniform splitting with the same budget optimizer and live-state costs, making them strong controls.

For factors, the required control is the strongest equivalent strip/residency implementation of the original dense graph, followed by ordinary optimized factor execution. Existing quality estimates belong to the frozen factors. Further rank or training selection must use an appropriate development split and a fresh evaluation protocol.

## Evidence levels

Native exact-output tests establish correctness for the tested arithmetic, inputs, and abstract RAM transactions. Synthesis and route establish implementation feasibility and timing for their pinned sources. Trace scheduling establishes behavior under its declared cost model. Neither sampled RAM stalls nor a finite arrival grid proves physical worst-case schedulability. Board and energy evaluation follow only if an implementation and controlled benefit survive this round.
